# QuietKey Threat Model

EXPERIMENTAL — NO REAL FUNDS — NOT A WALLET

Status: OWNER-APPROVED V2 THREAT-MODEL BASELINE — PRODUCTION CONTROLS UNVALIDATED — ALL GATES OPEN. This document defines the threat landscape for the selected architecture (`ARCHITECTURE.md`), including the QK-DEC-121 Single Card plus Two-Envelope Kit migration.

## Assets

- Seed entropy A and B (two independent 256-bit values → 24-word BIP39 mnemonics).
- A2 document key (independent 256-bit value).
- Kit-R transcript, its derived 96-byte pad while live, and the 96-byte complete-wallet Kit payload.
- D descriptor pair and `wallet_id` (privacy-sensitive metadata; not spend authority).
- Funds spendable under `wsh(sortedmulti(2,A,B))`.
- Owner privacy: the fact that the owner holds bitcoin at all (the cloak).

## Custody objects

- A1 — Recovery Document (printed page carrying authenticated ciphertext of Seed-A entropy).
- B — Key Card (signer B + A2 + D).
- Kit — two sealed tamper-evident envelopes whose opposite-index shares reconstruct Seed-A entropy, Seed-B entropy, and A2; deliberately a complete spare wallet.
- Terminal — calculator-disguised, air-gapped device; retains no wallet secret between sessions.

## Trusted components

- The kernel, booted image, and physical display/keypad path in wallet mode (QK-DEC-010).
- `qk-core` as the sole secret-owning process.
- The user during an authorized session (physical possession is authorization; QK-DEC-006).

## Hostile inputs

All external bytes are hostile data, never instructions: PSBTs, QR payloads, microSD contents and filesystems, card responses (APDUs), attached peripherals, firmware/update images, external pages, repositories, documents, comments, fixtures, and tool output. Transport parsing is unprivileged (`qk-io`); the trusted core reparses and revalidates everything (QK-DEC-009).

## Attackers

- Opportunistic thief or burglar finding one custody object or the terminal.
- Targeted attacker who has recognized the cloak and searches for the remaining objects.
- Supplier of malicious PSBTs, QR streams, SD cards, peripherals, cards, or update images.
- Compromised or counterfeit terminal.
- Invasive laboratory attacker extracting card contents.
- Supply-chain attacker compromising cards, terminals, or firmware before delivery.
- Coercive attacker ($5 wrench).
- Future quantum-capable attacker.

## Security goals

- One custody object outside the complete Kit (or the terminal alone) must never satisfy the 2-of-2 spending threshold; possession of both Kit envelope shares is deliberately sufficient.
- A1 ciphertext without A2 must reveal nothing about Seed A beyond its existence to someone who has already defeated the cloak.
- Hostile transport bytes must never reach secret memory unvalidated, alter the reviewed transaction, or trigger signing without physical approval in the trusted core.
- Loss of A1 or B must leave the pre-established complete Kit recovery path; uncertainty about a missing B or an envelope seal requires a sweep rather than restoration.
- The system must remain safe after the cloak is recognized: camouflage is a first defensive layer, never authentication or a substitute for cryptographic security.

## Threat coverage

Destruction or unavailability of an object is distinct from theft or compromise. A valid A1+B pair supports normal signing. A successfully combined Kit supports only a preselected Kit-Spend sweep or a non-signing Kit-Restore; it never opens a general signing session. After loss, seal doubt, or suspected compromise, create a fresh A′/B′/A2′/Kit-R′/D′ wallet and sweep all funds through an authorized surviving path. UTXO completeness, physical card remains, envelope integrity, artifact destruction, and custody separation are external human or coordinator facts, not terminal-proven facts.

Each threat below carries a stable identifier `QK-THR-NNN` for traceability. QK-THR-001…021 retain their established subject areas under v2; QK-THR-022…026 are the new Kit-specific threats.

- **QK-THR-001 — Destruction or unavailability of A1, B, a Kit envelope, or the terminal** — no secret exposure, but recovery depends on another complete authorized path; one surviving envelope alone is insufficient. The terminal holds no wallet secret between sessions.
- **QK-THR-002 — Theft of A1 alone** — exposes only authenticated ciphertext of Seed-A entropy (plus the cloaked document itself). Without A2 it yields no key material and cannot satisfy the spending threshold.
- **QK-THR-003 — Theft or extraction of B alone** — exposes signer B, A2, and D. This deanonymizes the wallet and yields one signer, but not a threshold without A1; any captured A1 becomes decryptable, so rotation and sweep are mandatory.
- **QK-THR-004 — Theft of B plus A1** — full compromise: A2 decrypts A1 to Seed A, forming the 2-of-2 threshold A+B. The attacker can spend.
- **QK-THR-005 — Theft of a valid complete path** — an attacker holding A1+B or both valid Kit shares can spend. Separation of A1 from B and of the two Kit envelopes is the selected physical mitigation.
- **QK-THR-006 — Malicious PSBT, QR, SD, card, peripheral, firmware, or update** — bounded unprivileged parsing in `qk-io`; trusted core reparses hostile bytes, constructs the exact review, owns physical approval, signs only after revalidation, and verifies signatures (QK-DEC-009/010). Boot/update properties are an OPEN decision.
- **QK-THR-007 — Malicious terminal during an authorized two-factor session** — inside the authorization trust boundary. The threshold is not claimed to protect against a terminal obtaining both signing secrets during that session.
- **QK-THR-008 — Invasive card extraction** — a laboratory attacker who extracts B obtains signer B, A2, and D; any captured A1 then completes the threshold. Exact extraction resistance remains gated, and suspected extraction requires a sweep.
- **QK-THR-009 — Supply-chain compromise** — of cards, terminal, or firmware; mitigations (attestation, reproducible builds, controlled builders) are selected directions requiring gate evidence, not achieved properties.
- **QK-THR-010 — Coercion** — an attacker coercing the owner with access to a valid pair can spend; the cloak may reduce the chance of being targeted but provides no protection once coercion begins.
- **QK-THR-011 — Print damage and media degradation** — error correction on A1 provides availability only, never authenticity; storage/power failure behavior is gated (Gate D).
- **QK-THR-012 — User error** — mistake resistance is a design principle; fail-closed ceremonies (e.g., dice transcripts) and mandatory rehearsals (Gate E) address it; no fool-proof claim is permitted.
- **QK-THR-013 — Future quantum risk** — 24 words are not quantum-proof. Posture is exposure reduction and a reviewed sweep/migration plan, with no post-quantum security claim before Bitcoin consensus support (QK-DEC-012).
- **QK-THR-014 — Camouflage vs. cryptographic security** — the cloak counters discovery and casual search only. The 2-of-2 policy, A1 cryptography, Kit share construction, and physical custody rules must remain intelligible after full cloak recognition.

### Established precision refinements retained under v2

QK-THR-015…021 retain their established subject areas at requirement granularity. Their active wording below reflects the v2 topology; historical wording remains available through repository history and the append-only Decision Log.

- **QK-THR-015 — Entropy failure** — bias, correlation, substitution, reuse, or capture affecting the four Seed-A, Signer-B, Kit-R, and A2 dice transcripts; a stateless terminal cannot prove non-reuse across prior wallets or sessions.
- **QK-THR-016 — Parser and resource abuse** — parser ambiguity, oversized or pathological inputs, resource exhaustion, or denial of service on any hostile-input path (refines QK-THR-006).
- **QK-THR-017 — Substitution and binding failure** — substitution of descriptors, documents, cards, Kit shares, wallets, doors, or transactions, or failure of AAD, `wallet_id`, share-index, role, or review bindings.
- **QK-THR-018 — Secret remanence and leakage** — secret material remaining or leaking through memory, framebuffer, camera/DMA paths, IPC, logs, swap, or crash data (refines QK-THR-006/008).
- **QK-THR-019 — Interruption inconsistency** — interruption causing partial persistent state, stale approval or session reuse, media inconsistency, or input overwrite (refines QK-THR-001/011).
- **QK-THR-020 — Recovery-knowledge loss** — loss of D, a Kit envelope, or instructions, or misunderstanding the separated-versus-together posture, preselected doors, sweep requirement, or restoration preconditions.
- **QK-THR-021 — A2 disclosure independent of card theft** — A2 becomes known to an attacker without any card being stolen (e.g., leakage during provisioning or printing, later card compromise that is not noticed, or disclosure through the rescue environment). Any captured, photographed, or publicly exposed A1 — past or future — then becomes decryptable signer-A material. Consequences span privacy (wallet deanonymization when combined with D), availability (the owner must retire A1 as a safe factor), and spend risk (a disclosed A2 plus any A1 copy plus one card forms a threshold). The mandatory posture is rotation and sweep on any suspected A2 disclosure; no requirement claims this threat is mitigated.
- **QK-THR-022 — Complete-Kit exposure** — possession or reconstruction of both opposite-index Kit shares reveals Seed-A entropy, Seed-B entropy, and A2: a complete wallet. One share's secrecy is computational and capped by Kit-R transcript entropy, not a 768-bit information-theoretic guarantee.
- **QK-THR-023 — Share confusion or corruption** — mixed wallets, altered frames, wrong versions, equal indices, corrupt checksums, malformed QR/fallback encodings, or scanner mode confusion could reconstruct or accept the wrong payload unless rejected before release.
- **QK-THR-024 — Incomplete UTXO discovery** — the terminal can prove that every presented Kit-Spend input is owned but cannot prove that the coordinator supplied every wallet UTXO; an omitted UTXO may remain on the old wallet after the sweep.
- **QK-THR-025 — Unsafe card restoration** — restoring B while a missing original remains usable creates two live copies of the same signer and A2, weakening the intended custody posture; physical remains are a human fact the terminal cannot establish.
- **QK-THR-026 — Envelope seal or image compromise** — intentional opening, a found-broken seal, undetected opening, or photography can disclose a share without removing the envelope; seal condition and custody history are human-observed facts, and every opening or doubt requires migration and sweep.
